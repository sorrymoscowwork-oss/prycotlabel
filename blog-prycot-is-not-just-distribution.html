PRYCOT. — Not Just Distribution

05.10.2026 / PRYCOT.

PRYCOT. began with a simple idea: make it easier for independent musicians to bring their work into the world.

A musician writes a song, records it, mixes it, creates the artwork — and then enters a space where the music itself almost disappears. Forms, platforms, metadata, payments, intermediaries. Music becomes an object that has to be properly packaged and delivered.

We were interested in the opposite: keeping a place for the musician inside that system.

That is how PRYCOT. began.

Two sides of the same idea

Today, the project has two sides that gradually became one.

The first is digital distribution. We help independent artists release music on platforms around the world. We work with an international audience and use cryptocurrency for payments and settlements with artists.

This is an important part of how we operate, but it is not its meaning.

For us, cryptocurrency is closer to new infrastructure than an ideology. It makes it possible to work with an artist in another country without the familiar chain of financial intermediaries, to pay for a release and receive royalties in digital currency.

But PRYCOT. did not come from an interest in financial technology.

It came from music.

Music instead of a conveyor belt

We write music and lyrics ourselves. We work with musicians and bands. We produce artists and develop our own projects. We already work with musicians from different countries.

That is why the word “distributor” has always felt too narrow.

A distributor sees a release. We are more interested in seeing the person who made it.

We want to understand why this music exists, what is personal about it, where it might go. Sometimes that simply means helping an artist release finished work. Sometimes it means guiding them through the entire process. And sometimes a conversation that begins with a single song gradually turns into a collaboration.

For us, that is the difference between delivering music and working with music.

Not everything can be scaled

The modern music industry is very good at working with quantity. Thousands of releases, millions of files, automated checks, algorithms, catalogues.

It is an efficient machine.

But art is generally difficult to reconcile with industrial production.

We prefer another pace — one that allows us to stop at a single song, hear it several times, talk to its author, understand what works and what is still looking for its form.

We do not want to become a large service. What matters more is preserving the ability to work attentively.

We want a young musician from any country to have a simple path into the world’s digital infrastructure — without unnecessary complexity and without having to become an expert in the music industry.

But behind that simple system there is another possibility: if the music genuinely interests us, the work can continue.

Not as a service.

As a collaboration.

Two sides of the same project

That is how the two directions of PRYCOT. came together.

On one side — Web3 distribution: international music releases, payment and settlements in cryptocurrency, modern digital infrastructure.

On the other — the music label.

The first helps music reach the world.

The second answers the question of which music we want to bring there.

And that, perhaps, is the most important thing.

PRYCOT. was not created to process as many releases as possible. We are more interested in a few artists we can genuinely work with than in a stream of anonymous files.

We are not trying to separate art from technology. Quite the opposite — technology should disappear where music begins.

If everything works properly, an artist simply releases their work and continues doing what they started making it for in the first place.

And we remain somewhere between a studio, a label and digital infrastructure.

Small enough to hear an individual person.

And technological enough to help their music go anywhere.
